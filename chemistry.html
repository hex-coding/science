1. States of Matter & Particle Model

Everything around you is made of matter (has mass and takes up space). It changes state when energy (heat) is added or removed.

The Particle Model:
  • All matter is made of tiny particles.
  • Particles are constantly moving.
  • There are spaces between particles.
  • Heating makes particles move faster and spread apart; cooling does the opposite.

2. Physical Changes

In a physical change, appearance or state changes, but no new substance is formed. Most are reversible.

Examples:
  • Tearing paper
  • Stretching an elastic band
  • Dissolving salt in water
  • Crushing ice
✨ Dig Deeper: Test rule: Could you theoretically get the original back using only physical processes (filtering, melting, freezing)? If yes = physical change!

3. Chemical Changes

Particles rearrange to form new substances. Usually irreversible.

Signs of Chemical Change:
  • Colour change
  • Gas produced (bubbles/fizz)
  • New smell
  • Light given off
  • Temperature change
  • Solid (precipitate) forms
✨ Dig Deeper: You don't need all 6 signs! Even 1 clear sign can prove a chemical change occurred.

4. Corrosion & Preventing Rust

Rust Equation: Iron + Oxygen + Water → Rust (Iron Oxide)

Prevention Methods:
  • Galvanising: Coating iron/steel with zinc.
  • Painting / Oiling: Creates a physical barrier against air and water.
  • Cathodic Protection: Attaching a more reactive "sacrificial" metal.
  • Alloying: Mixing iron with chromium/nickel to form stainless steel.
  • Humidity Control: Keeping environments dry.

5. Solubility & Solutions
  • Solute: Substance that dissolves.
  • Solvent: Liquid that does the dissolving.
  • Solution: Solute + Solvent mixture.
  • Saturated: Can't dissolve any more solute at that temperature.
Dissolving factors:
  • Higher Temp: Dissolves solids faster/more. (Gases behave opposite! Warm drinks go flat faster).
  • Stirring: Speeds up process.
  • Smaller Particles: Dissolves faster due to greater surface area.
